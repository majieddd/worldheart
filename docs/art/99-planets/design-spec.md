# 99 Planets To Defend: shared direction brief

## Intent

Worldheart is becoming 99 Planets To Defend. The owner wants the game's actual
visual and design foundation, not another unrelated fantasy artbook. The existing
illustrated board is retained as a possible medieval-planet study. Its weapons
and individually composed concept sheets are useful references, but its generic
editorial web layout, slogans, fantasy scenery and mixed HUD/loadout composition
are not the new game's identity. A player should recognize a playful, dangerous,
contemporary Roblox crossover adventure before reading any explanation.

The game starts on present-day Earth, on Friday 13 April 2029. Its grounded
opening turns into an alien invasion when humanity fires on the approaching
object. The player is a sleeper agent activated to defend Earth. The real event
is Apophis's safe flyby on that date, not an asteroid entering Earth orbit or a
predicted impact. The interception and aliens are clearly the fictional branch.
The emotional model is an earnest adventure with absurd crossover characters:
serious consequences, brief goofy details, and enough visual calm that both can
work. Do not make either a military simulator or an all-jokes meme collage.

The art foundation is the owner's Paintline comparison column and Inked Cel
posters and models. These have crisp dark silhouettes, painted local color,
meaningful material wear and clean deep colored shadows. The owner specifically
does not want the posters' line-filled shadow hatching in 3D. The UI needs the
same clarity as a real Roblox game screenshot: comfortable block-scale type,
simple functional shapes, small essential controls and generous visible world.
The illustrations provide texture. The interface must not add fake paper grain,
thick ornamental frames, random stickers, invented metrics or marketing slogans.

## What exists now, observed this session

The public main game was replayed in a fresh browser profile, not inferred from
old screenshots. The lobby exposes Commanders, Tower foundry, Mounts, Homeworld
and Mission gate. A normal expedition has 10 waves on a planet; the longer
campaign has 99 planets. A first attempt placed Bolt Sentinel defenses and ended
on wave four with 25 kills. A retry entered commander control, moved, jumped,
used Cyclone Slash and Aegis Ward, and opened the current weapon loadout. The
source model gallery was inspected for Bulwark, Longsight and Bolt Sentinel.
This was a bounded reference replay, not a balance or full-campaign certification.

The worldheart, commander health, wave state, base radius and upgrade, crystal
deposit, construction economy, weapon equipment, extracted loot and abilities
are genuine game concepts. The production equipment model has two equipped
slots, a backpack and no ammunition/reload counter. Do not invent a bullet
counter, battle pass, daily reward, multiplayer leaderboard or unrelated HUD
systems. Current source values may be used as clearly staged concept states:
commander 1400 health, Worldheart 20, Bolt Sentinel construction cost 150,
crystals 0/3, Aegis Ward and Cyclone Slash. A UI concept is not a live build.

## Five owner-defined factions

1. Native alien invader: undecipherable fictional Adamic-inspired glyphs encoding
   `ayy lmao`. Do not assert an authentic Adamic translation. Material vocabulary:
   rust chitin plates, ivory hooked brow, lime capillary channels. The invaders
   arrive in meteor shells. Heavy and skitterer silhouettes share those materials.
2. Brainshot: Brainrot-inspired equipment. Humor belongs in the construction:
   shark/sneaker plasma carbine, banana blade, espresso thermal staff. Materials:
   sticker-cut enamel, exposed screws, rubber grip tape. No generic meme-word soup.
3. RainBOOM: Rainbow Friends-inspired weapons specializing in color explosions.
   Cobalt plastic armor, button-eye power gauge, separate colored pigment chambers,
   crown antenna and creature-jaw muzzle. Separate color fragments, no rainbow fog.
4. Anomalous: condition-triggered effects. Smoked glass cages, off-register ivory
   plates, amber witness lamps. Tell the player the condition and whether it is
   dormant or armed. The exact mechanics are proposed; no new balance is shipped.
5. Axiom: neutral autonomous robots maintaining universal balance. The accepted
   animated robot is now part of this faction. Preserve its squat broad ochre
   head, binocular black eyes, heavy brows, tapered torso and visible limb joints.
   Equipment shares ochre ceramic panels, graphite spine and twin teal lamps.

These are faction names supplied by the owner. New weapon-specific names are
not needed; use family labels. Do not reuse Playtime, Latchworks or Tideforge.
These illustrations are original faction interpretations, not official IP logos
or licensed crossover announcements. The page may state the supplied inspirations.

## Shared asset kit

All assets below exist under `lib/99-art/`; use them rather than drawing fake 3D
scenes in CSS. `earth.png` is the common aspirational modern-Earth gameplay
background for the three UI studies. `alien.png`, `brainshot.png`, `rainboom.png`,
`anomalous.png`, `axiom.png` are faction plates. PNG originals are full resolution;
optimized WebP siblings are being made for display. Use either existing sibling.
`references/` holds actual public game captures and the binding source paintings.
`fonts/` contains licensed Barlow Regular, SemiBold and Barlow Condensed Bold with
OFL files. Fonts are local and work without remote CDN access.

## Output and comparison contract

Create a real self-contained HTML/CSS/JS first draft at the assigned file inside
`design-demos/99-planets/`. Primary comparison viewport: **1440 x 900**. A 16:9
game viewport should occupy most of the page below a restrained review toolbar.
Keep a small outside-the-game label saying this is a UI concept. Inside the
viewport, no design explanation, placeholder prose or marketing heading.
The scene needs to look like a game screenshot with a usable HUD. At 390px,
controls and text remain accessible; a deliberate scaled scene is acceptable,
but surrounding page must not overflow. Use touch-friendly controls at least
44px high. Contrast: 4.5:1 body text, 3:1 large text. Honor reduced motion.

Provide functional switches for Combat / Build / Loadout. Combat favors aim,
health, wave objective, two weapon slots and Z/V ability hints. Build reveals
existing tower choices and cost/placement feedback. Loadout is a separate full
game state with two equipment slots and manufacturer context, not a giant menu
permanently covering combat. All numbers are staged concept values, not telemetry.
At least one faction selection must visibly update equipment/identity. The
provided raster plates may be shown uncut on a distinct reference page/state;
do not crop arbitrary weapon icons out of a plate if their crop is unreliable.
Do not create actual new gameplay, storage migrations or backend services.

Brand kit in the draft: a compact proposed title treatment for `99 PLANETS TO
DEFEND`, two/three functional UI colors sampled from the art, clear type hierarchy,
and one distinctive device derived from the actual game, such as orbit/base
coverage, a planetary count or a readable control strip. Avoid giant decorative
99, slanted neon SaaS cards, generic hero/subtitle/three-features marketing layout,
serif artbook captions and faux military dossier clutter. The owner disliked
the previous board's AI-generated feel. Every visible element must earn its place.

Each direction must be independent and meaningfully different in composition,
not only recolored. Do not inspect the other agents' drafts. Keep changes to the
assigned files. No em dash. Do not publish or commit; parent handles integration.
Use links to `../../99-art.html` for the shared concept collection and
`../../index.html` for the actual game. Maintain the art direction across drafts;
the choice is interface/brand composition, not a re-vote on Paintline.
